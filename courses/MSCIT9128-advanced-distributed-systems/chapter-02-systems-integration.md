# Chapter 2 — Systems integration (silos → ERP)

Use this when Section B mentions SwiftTrade, AgriMart “separate systems”, or POSDCORB.

## Functional silos

A silo is a department that works as if the rest of the firm did not exist. Two cuts:

- **Horizontal:** work split by function (accounting, HR, sales, warehouse) — the POSDCORB split (Planning, Organising, Staffing, Directing, Coordinating, Reporting, Budgeting).
- **Vertical:** work split by rank (strategy at the top, tactics in the middle, daily operations at the bottom).

Silo symptoms in a CAT case: sales cannot see stock; accounting re-types warehouse invoices; each team guards its own files.

## Why integrate

The business process (order → pick → invoice → cash) crosses departments. Integration means **one process and one set of figures**, not five packages that never agree.

Two layers of integration:

- **Logical:** shared meaning (one customer ID, one price list, one stock figure).
- **Physical:** the actual links (middleware, APIs, EDI/B2B, or a single ERP database).

Before ERP, firms often need to retire or wrap legacy systems and upgrade networks. After ERP, the goal is organisational (one process) more than departmental (one team’s spreadsheet).

## Management issues

- Who owns master data (price, customer, SKU)?
- Ethical use of a newly joined data set (staff must not exploit access).
- Training, firewalls, and an acceptable-use policy — integration without security just makes a bigger target.
